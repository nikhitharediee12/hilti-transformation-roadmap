Why each Accountable role carries the decision

#AccountableRationale
D1Chief Executive OfficerFour of the six Executive Board members represent a business area or market that competes for the same funds (S17). The CEO is the only member without a portfolio to defend, so the CEO can trade software investment against hardware and market reach. The finance member prepares the allocation but should not also own it.
D2Group CIOHilti's CIO publicly owns the architecture and security principles and reports only one modification to SAP standard code (S4). That discipline is what keeps one global instance possible, so the person who set the rule must grant the exceptions. Business sponsors are Consulted so they argue their case before the decision, not after.
D3Executive Board Member, Finance, Tax, HR & ITData access policy is written into customer contracts: Hilti's ON!Track terms govern API credentials and the developer agreement (S9), so it carries legal and commercial exposure beyond IT. The Executive Board member who holds IT together with finance and HR (S15) can set a policy that binds every product team; the CIO implements it.
D4Executive Board Member, Electric Tools & Accessories and Corporate ResearchThis member publicly led the 4PS acquisition and explained the software portfolio logic (S5, S12). We read that as ownership of the software portfolio; this is team inference, because Hilti does not publish who owns the software P&L. Prioritizing across products needs one owner above the product teams; market heads are Consulted because they compete for which region gets features first.
D5Group CIOEvery such partner creates a data interface that must meet the same architecture and security principles the CIO enforces (S4). Hilti pre-selects the third-party apps it integrates (S9), so someone must own that choice. Giving it to the CIO keeps partner selection and integration standards together; business and finance leads are Consulted on value and terms.
D6Chief Executive OfficerDirect customer relationships are one of Hilti's two named strategic choices (S11), and a software credit rule touches both market regions and every business area. Only the CEO sits above all of them; if one market head owned it, each region would set its own rule.
D7Executive Board Member, Electric Tools & Accessories and Corporate ResearchHilti kept 4PS's team, management and brand (S12, S14), a deliberate light-integration choice that will be revisited as the portfolio grows. The executive who sponsored the acquisition owns the outcome. The CIO is Consulted because integration depth decides whether D2 exceptions are needed.

What makes each decision-right real when nobody is looking

A RACI is only as real as the mechanism that enforces it, the same way our repository's branch protection refuses an unreviewed merge. These mechanisms are our proposals, not documented Hilti practice.

#DecisionProposed enforcement (team analysis)
D1Budget allocationFunding for a Run initiative is released only through the annual plan the CEO signs; no market can self-fund a Run rollout.
D2Architecture standardsAny change to SAP standard code needs a CIO-approved change record before it can be moved into production.
D3Data governanceAPI credentials are issued only under the published ON!Track terms (S9); new data-sharing uses require a terms update the Accountable signs.
D4Initiative prioritizationOne shared software backlog with a single ranking; teams cannot pull work that is not ranked.
D5Vendor & partnershipA partner appears as a Hilti-provided integration only after a CIO-approved listing review.
D6Case-specific: software selling in direct salesSales-plan templates for the year cannot be issued until the software credit rule is set.
D7Case-specific: acquired-software integrationEach acquisition closes with a written integration charter, reviewed at every QBR.
