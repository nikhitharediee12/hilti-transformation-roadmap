Crawl, Walk, Run: Hilti's software build-out

Seven initiatives from Hilti's move into construction software, sequenced by what each one makes possible. Every company fact links to a source; every sequencing argument is marked as our analysis.

Owner: Chandrahas Reddy Bheemireddy (Roadmap Sequencer)

Two timelines, read them separately

Hilti actual (verified). Hilti built the three Crawl capabilities as separate historical waves: ON!Track in 2015, the S/4HANA core in 2017 to 2018, and the Fieldwire acquisition in 2021. That is about six years, far outside the brief's 6 to 12 month Crawl phase, so we do not present it as an execution plan.

Execution horizon (plan, team reconstruction). The same seven initiatives and dependencies, re-planned as one program: Crawl in quarters 1 to 4, Walk in 5 to 7, and Run from quarter 8 onward (12+ months, ongoing). This is the roadmap a leadership team would execute and track; every initiative carries both its plan quarters and its verified Hilti dates.

Scope. The roadmap covers Hilti's software and connected-jobsite transformation from 2015 to 2023, the period between its digital core and its Lead 2030 software portfolio. Fleet Management, Hilti's tool-leasing model from 2000-2001 (S18, S19), is the business context but sits outside the window.

Phase rationale

Crawl: why it must come first

Each Walk initiative consumes something only a Crawl initiative creates. The ON!Track Unite Pilot exposes asset data through an API, so ON!Track must first hold that data in the cloud. The Fieldwire Europe Launch needs a product to sell, which the acquisition supplies, and is easier to scale when software is billed through the standard S/4HANA order-to-cash. The three Crawl initiatives do not depend on each other, so in the execution horizon they run in parallel within quarters 1 to 4, even though Hilti historically built them as separate waves.

Team analysis. 90 words.

Walk: why it must come before Run

Both Walk initiatives test something Run would otherwise bet the whole company on. The Unite pilot was limited to the US and Canada, so Hilti could learn whether customers actually connect integrations and how much implementation support each one needs before every market organization staffs that service. The Fieldwire Europe Launch tests whether a direct sales force built for tools can sell software subscriptions outside the US. Scaling before these signals arrive would multiply an unproven service model, and buying a larger European software business would add complexity first.

Team analysis. 89 words.

Run: why it must come last

Run initiatives come last because they change the organization, not only the technology. The global Unite rollout requires every market organization to deliver integration services, which Hilti later wrote into its standard ON!Track terms (2024 version). The integrated portfolio asks account managers to sell several software products alongside tools and Fleet Management, and asks Hilti to absorb a 350-person company built on a different technology stack. Those changes are worth their disruption only after Walk shows adoption and revenue, so Run starts behind a gate rather than on a date.

Team analysis. 90 words.

What the public record does not tell us

  • Hilti is privately owned and publishes no digital transformation KPIs; all dashboard values except M1 are team illustrations.
  • The roadmap shows two timelines. Hilti's historical capability waves (2015 to 2023) are verified dates; the execution horizon (Crawl Q1 to Q4, Walk Q5 to Q7, Run Q8 onward) is our reconstruction of how the same dependency chain would be run as one program.
  • Most sources are written by Hilti or its technology vendors (SAP, Microsoft). We found little independent critique of Hilti's transformation.
  • Launch quarters for ON!Track (2015) and the Fieldwire Europe Launch (2022) are not published.
  • The go-live date of SAP Subscription Billing on SAP BTP, and whether it bills Fieldwire in Europe, are not published; dependency 1 is therefore an enabling dependency, not a proven prerequisite.
  • Hilti does not publish who owns the software P&L. Assigning D4 and D7 to the Executive Board member for Electric Tools & Accessories is our inference from his public role in the 4PS acquisition.
  • The QBR is set in Q4 2023, after Hilti's January 2023 leadership change, so every role matches the Executive Board structure Hilti reported for 2023 (S17). The title of the second markets seat (EMEA Markets) comes from Hilti's current site (S16); S17 confirms two market seats in 2023.
  • Fleet Management start dates conflict: an HBS case says 2000 (S18), an IMD case says the Swiss market launched it in 2001 (S19). Fleet Management sits outside our roadmap window and is used as context only.
